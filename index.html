01 THE ESSENTIALS

At a glance

The EE is a student-led investigation of personal interest, using one DP subject or integrating two subjects when both are needed. It is compulsory for full Diploma students and optional for course students. [official 2027 guide]

i

Two parts, one process. The essay is a formal academic paper of up to 4,000 words. The Research and Progress Form (RPF) carries one reflective statement of up to 500 words. The reflection is assessed separately under Criterion E.

The EE is externally assessed. The IB estimates about 40 hours of work and recommends 3–5 hours of supervision. The EE and TOK contribute up to 3 Diploma points; a D or higher in the EE is needed for the Diploma.

02 THE 2027 RESET

What changes for 2027?

The first-assessment-2027 guide reframes how students plan, how examiners award marks and how reflection is evidenced. Here are the shifts that matter most in practice.

01

A 30-mark A–E rubric

The 2027 criteria total 30 marks: A–C 6 each, D 8 and E 4. See the official rubric below. [official guide]

30 marks
02

Two explicit ways to frame the inquiry

Students choose a subject-focused pathway or an interdisciplinary pathway. Interdisciplinary work must integrate two DP subjects so the combination contributes insight that one subject alone would not offer.

Two pathways
03

Reflection becomes one evaluated statement

Three reflection sessions remain mandatory. The student submits one reflective statement of up to 500 words on the RPF, and reflection and learning are assessed under Criterion E.

RPF · 500 words
04

Discussion and evaluation carry more weight

Criterion D is worth 8 marks. Discuss findings with evidence and evaluate the essay’s effectiveness, including strengths and limitations—not general presentation.

Criterion D · 8
05

Check your subject’s current guide

Subject-specific requirements vary. Confirm the rules for your subject and exam session before finalizing your research question.

Check your subject
Use the right version. This page summarizes the IB’s official first-assessment-2027 guide. Subject-specific rules can vary; check the current guide for your subject and exam session.

03 HOW THE MARKS WORK

Assessment: 30 marks

A–D assess the essay as a whole. E assesses only the RPF reflective statement. Examiners use best fit: the descriptors guide judgement, not a checklist.

A

Framework for the essay 6 marks

Does the research question, the research methods and the structure provide an effective framework?

Research question · Research methods · Structure
Open mark bands by subcriterion

1–2 marks

Research question

Stated, but lacks relevance to the investigation, clarity or focus.

Research methods

Used, but mostly unsuitable for the research question.

Structure

Structural conventions are present but do not support communication of the research.

3–4 marks

Research question

Relevant and clear, but only partly focused for the essay’s scope.

Research methods

Mostly suitable methods are explained and applied with partial effectiveness.

Structural conventions support some aspects of communication.

5–6 marks

Research question

Relevant to the investigation, clear and focused for the essay’s scope.

Research methods

Knowledge and understanding 6 marks

Knowledge · Understanding—Terminology · Understanding—Concepts
Open mark bands by subcriterion

1–2 marks

Knowledge

Research materials are used to establish knowledge but lack relevance.

Understanding — Terminology

Terminology is used, but accuracy is weak or use is very limited.

Understanding — Concepts

Concepts are identified but irrelevant or explained only superficially.

3–4 marks

Relevant research materials establish some knowledge of the subject.

Understanding — Terminology

Some relevant terminology is accurate and demonstrates subject understanding.

5–6 marks

Knowledge

Comprehensive, relevant materials establish subject knowledge.

Understanding — Terminology

Relevant terminology is accurate and consistently shows understanding.

Understanding — Concepts

Relevant concepts are explained and used effectively to demonstrate understanding.

Analysis · Line of argument
Open mark bands by subcriterion

Analysis

Line of argument

A partial line of argument is present.

3–4 marks

Analysis

Partly effective analysis produces some relevant findings.

5–6 marks

Analysis

Effective, consistent analysis produces relevant findings.

Discussion and evaluation 8 marks

Are findings discussed with evidence, and is the essay’s effectiveness evaluated?

Discussion · Evaluation
Open mark bands by subcriterion

1–2 marks

Discussion

Findings’ significance is described without reference to supporting evidence.

Evaluation

Evaluation of the essay’s effectiveness is partial; strengths and limitations are not stated.

3–4 marks

Discussion

Discussion of significance is partly balanced and sometimes supported by appropriate evidence.

Evaluation

Evaluation is present, with some strengths and limitations stated.

5–6 marks

Discussion

Discussion is balanced and often supported by appropriate evidence.

Evaluation

Relevant strengths and limitations are described.

7–8 marks

Discussion

Discussion of significance is balanced and fully supported by appropriate evidence.

Evaluation

Relevant strengths and limitations are explained.

Open mark bands by subcriterion

Growth

2 marks

Evaluative process

Growth

Growth

Evaluative process

These are concise paraphrases of the official rubric, not a replacement for it. A criterion can score 0 if the work is below its lowest band; a high mark does not require faultless work. Official guide, pp. 112–114 ↗

Do not mix up criteria: presentation conventions are part of A’s framework; D is discussion and evaluation.

04 CHOOSE YOUR LENS

One subject—or two?

Choose the pathway that best answers the question. Begin with the student’s interest, then test whether one subject offers the strongest lens or whether two subjects truly need to work together.

PATH 01

Subject-focused

One DP subject supplies the concepts, knowledge, perspectives and methods used to investigate the question. Keep the topic and analysis within that subject’s academic boundaries.

Good fit when: one discipline can answer the question well on its own.

PATH 02

Interdisciplinary

Two DP subjects are integrated because their combination helps answer the question and creates insight a single-subject approach would not provide. Balance can vary, but integration must be visible.

Good fit when: the question needs the perspectives, concepts, theories or methods of both subjects.

THE FIVE INTERDISCIPLINARY FRAMEWORKS

Power, equality, justiceCulture, identity, expressionMovement, time, spaceEvidence, measurement, innovationSustainability, development, change

A topic can connect to more than one framework. It does not need to cover every word in the chosen framework. Environmental systems and societies (ESS) and Literature and performance use the subject-focused pathway.

05 FROM INTEREST TO INQUIRY

Shape a question you can answer

A focused research question narrows an interesting subject into a manageable investigation. It should invite analysis, evaluation and synthesis—not a story or a list of facts.

Before you commit, ask:

  • Is it clearly connected to the chosen DP subject or subjects?
  • Can it be answered within 4,000 words?
  • Can I access enough reliable sources and use a suitable method in time?
  • Can I make a reasoned argument rather than simply describe?
  • Is it ethical and safe to research in the proposed way?
  • Could the answer change when I examine the evidence?
BEFORE → MORE FOCUSED

Examples to discuss—not questions to copy.

“What was the impact of Ho Chi Minh’s allegiance to Lenin?”

→

“To what extent was nationalism the guiding factor in Ho Chi Minh’s adoption of Leninism in 1920?”

“What is the history of Chinese theatre?”

→

“How does the legacy of Mei Lan Fang contribute to modern Jingju?”

“How important is chlorophyll to plant life?”

→

“What is the effect of different concentrations of kinetin on leaves aging and the biosynthesis of chlorophyll?”

“How can the US government’s spending policy be reformed?”

→

“To what extent did the rising COE prices affect the demand for new and used cars by the consumer population and hence affect the revenue generated by the Singaporean economy for the period 2012–16?”

?

Plan the method: methodology is the overall strategy and why it fits; methods are the steps or tools. Describe them clearly enough to evaluate. Before practical work, check safety, consent and school approvals.

06 FOLLOW THE EVIDENCE

Research, then reason

A strong essay does more than gather sources. It evaluates the evidence, compares perspectives, develops findings and uses them to sustain a clear line of argument.

IN EE RESEARCH TERMS

Primary research

First-hand data gathered by the student, such as experiments, interviews or fieldwork. Not every subject permits it; check subject rules and ethics first.

IN EE RESEARCH TERMS

Secondary research

Analysis of existing sources and data. Every EE needs secondary research; in science, suitable secondary data can work when subject rules permit and the analysis is rigorous.

!

Keep research traceable. A diary is a primary source; primary research means data you collect first-hand. Ask a librarian about databases, and log searches, key sources and decisions.

Subject-specific example · Music and cultural research

Use these prompts alongside the ethical expectations for your subject and community; they are not a universal checklist.

  • Respect context: honour cultural ownership, traditions and meanings, especially when working with marginalized or Indigenous communities.
  • Represent accurately: do not distort a musical practice, work or interpretation to fit a thesis.
  • Credit and cite: acknowledge composers and performers; reference scores, recordings, articles and ideas.
  • Respect rights: use music, lyrics and recordings only with permission or an applicable copyright exception, following local law and school policy.

Read each source with a question in mind

Who created it—and why?What method or evidence supports it?Whose perspective is missing?How relevant and reliable is it for my question?What are its strengths and limitations?

A USEFUL PARAGRAPH RHYTHM

Make the reasoning visible

Build paragraphs around one claim. Bring in relevant evidence, explain what that evidence supports, link the point to the research question, and connect it to the next step in the argument.

01

Introduction

Set the topic and context, establish the focus, state the research question and orient the reader to the approach.

02

Body

Develop one main point at a time. Analyse relevant evidence and keep each section connected to the question.

03

Conclusion

Answer the research question and bring together the main findings. Do not introduce new evidence or arguments here.

Reflect, meet, revise

02

03

Evaluate learning; the supervisor probes understanding.

One short statement. Real learning.

08 AUTHENTICITY & AI

Use AI to learn—not to author

A practical test: is the tool helping the student learn, or claiming thinking, writing or experience that is not theirs? Be transparent, verify sources and follow the school’s and IB’s current rules.

!

Official IB guide: AI may support research when used permissibly, checked against reliable sources and acknowledged/cited. Submitting AI-written work as your own is dishonest; follow your school’s rules for permitted use. IB guide, pp. 93–94 · IB AI guidance ↗

Keep an evidence trail. Save drafts and research notes. Verify AI output against original sources, cite material used and make sure the final question, reasoning and reflection are your own.

09 GUIDE WITHOUT TAKING OVER

The supervisor’s role

Agree realistic milestones and meet early once a draft question is ready. Help students explain their own decisions; do not supply their argument or writing.

Helpful supervisor moves

  • Check the question, method and scope.
  • Ask open questions; agree a next step.
  • Point to current subject and ethics guidance.
  • Use the viva voce to check the student’s understanding.

TRY ASKING

“What did the evidence change?”

“Why does this method fit?”

Feedback boundary: identify areas to improve, but do not rewrite, line-edit or dictate exact changes. Follow the current draft-feedback limit; the school supervisor remains accountable if an external mentor helps.

PUBLIC STUDENT DOWNLOAD · NO PASSWORD REQUIRED

Extended Essay 2027 master checklist

Download this printable two-page checklist to review your pathway and research question, criteria A–E, Reflective Statement, formatting and final submission before hand-in.

Download checklist (PDF) ↓
01

Question & argument

Does the conclusion answer the research question? Does each section add to one coherent line of reasoning?

02

Evidence & citations

Use one referencing system consistently. Cite claims, data and visuals; include figures only when they help explain the argument.

03

Research quality

Have you considered source relevance, reliability, perspective, method, strengths and limitations?

04

Required structure

The title page needs the student code, research question, subject(s)/framework if interdisciplinary, and word count. Then include contents, introduction, body, conclusion and references or bibliography.

05

Format & anonymity

Use 12-point type and 1.5 line spacing. Number pages from the first page after the contents. No student, supervisor or school name anywhere in the file. Proofread the finished document.

06

Word count & file

Maximum 4,000 words; examiners stop at 4,000, and excess can compromise all criteria. Count introduction, body, conclusion, quotations and non-reference footnotes/endnotes. Exclude contents, headings, maps/charts/diagrams, tables, equations/formulas/calculations, citations/references, bibliography and RPF. Keep the essay file under 10 MB; upload the RPF separately.

Official guide, pp. 87–89. These are the IB’s core submission rules; check the current subject guidance for any additional conventions. Open source notes ↗

ONE LAST THOUGHT

Make the work yours.

A good EE is more than a long paper. It is a focused question, a defensible method, an argument built from evidence and an honest account of what the student learned.
